Insolvency Statistics in Canada—2024

Volume of Insolvency Filings

In 2024, a total of 143,483 insolvencies under the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act (BIA) were filed with the Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy (OSB). This represents a 12.1% increase from 2023. 

In addition there were 74 proceedings filed under the Companies’ Creditors Arrangement Act (CCAA) and 743 receiverships. 

BIA Insolvencies

The total volume of BIA insolvencies in 2024 has risen compared to 2021, 2022 and 2023 and has slightly surpassed the volume of insolvencies filed in the pre-pandemic year 2019. Consumer insolvencies increased from 123,233 fillings in 2023 to 137,295 fillings in 2024. The share of proposals among consumer insolvency filings had minimal change by 0.1% from 78.7% in 2023 to 78.8% in 2024. Business insolvencies increased from 4,810 in 2023 to 6,188 in 2024. The number of business insolvencies in 2024 constitutes the highest volume since 2010.

Note that…

In 2024, the Canadian real Gross Domestic Product (GDP) increased by 1.3%.Footnote Footnote 1. The unemployment rate increased from 5.4% in 2023 to 6.3% in 2024, and the participation rate decreased slightly from 65.8% in 2023 to 65.5% in 2024.Footnote Footnote 2 The Consumer Price Index increased by 2.4% in 2024. The preceding year saw the Consumer Price Index increase by 3.9%.Footnote Footnote 3

Over the course of 2024, the Bank of Canada decreased the overnight interest rate from 5% in January to 3.25% in December.Footnote Footnote 4 This 1.75% decrease in 2024 is the first time there has been a decrease since 2020. The household debt service ratio maintained a rate of 15.0% from 2023 to 2024.Footnote Footnote 5

Demographics at a Glance

Changes in the volume of BIA insolvencies filed by consumers were fairly small across all age groups in 2024. 

Consumers aged 35 to 49 made up 37.8% of insolvency fillings in 2024, the largest percentage share of all age groups. The change in the percentage share of insolvency filings from 2023 to 2024 is as follows:

  • Ages 18 to 34: decrease from 26.5% to 26.2%
  • Ages 35 to 49: increase from 37.5% to 37.8%
  • Ages 50 to 64: decrease from 25.3% to 25.0%
  • Ages 65 and over: increase from 10.8% to 11.0%
Note that…

Statistics Canada estimated Canada’s population to be 41,288,599 on July 1, 2024, an increase of 1,205,115 from the previous year. This represents a growth rate of 2.9%, in 2024. The 15 to 34-year-old group experienced a growth rate of 4.7%, the highest growth rate of all the age groups. Other age groups experienced a smaller  growth rate in 2024 with the exception of 50-64 which decreased by 0.13%.Footnote Footnote 6

Regions at a Glance

From 2023 to 2024, all provinces and territories saw an increase in BIA consumer insolvencies except for Nunavut and Newfoundland and Labrador, which saw a decrease of 26.7% and 0.6% respectively. Ontario and Quebec experienced the largest increases in consumer insolvencies with 17.8% and 12.1% respectively. Prince Edward Island and the Northwest Territories had the third and fourth largest increases at 11.4% and 9.8%.  

The provinces with the largest increases in the volume of consumer insolvencies were:

  • Ontario: 7806 more filings (17.8%)
  • Quebec: 3713 more filings (12.1%)
  • Alberta: 1069 more filings (6.0%)

In 2024, all provinces and territories experienced an increase in BIA business insolvencies, except Newfoundland and Labrador, Saskatchewan, and the Northwest Territories. The provinces that saw the largest increases in business insolvencies were:

  • Prince Edward Island : 1100% (11)
  • Ontario: 46.3% (593)
  • Nova Scotia: 42.9% (18)

The three provinces with the largest increases in the volume of business insolvencies were:

  • Quebec: 653 more filings (23.2%)
  • Ontario: 593 more filings (46.3%)
  • British Columbia: 51 more filings (20.2%)

Industry at a Glance

The total number of BIA insolvencies filed by businesses in 2024 increased by 28.6% from 2023. 

The three sectors that registered the largest increases in the number of insolvencies were:

  • Construction: 205 more filings (30.1%)
  • Transportation and warehousing: 198 more filings (58.6%)
  • Accommodation and food services: 163 more filings (22.7%)

The only sector that saw a decrease in the number of insolvencies in 2024 was Mining, quarrying, and oil and gas extraction. It had a 33.3% decrease over the course of a year.

There was a 15.6% increase in the volume of CCAA filings as compared to 2023.

In 2024 the three sectors with the greatest volume of CCAA filings were:

  • Manufacturing: 12 filings 
  • Wholesale Trade: 11 filings 
  • Agriculture, forestry, fishing and hunting: 10 filings

Note that more than one NAICS code can be reported in a CCAA filing, and this is reflected in the figures shown.

Note that…

In 2024, many sectors saw an increase in GDP growth rate. The sectors with the highest increases in GDP growth rate in 2024 were: 

  • Educational services: 4.4%
  • Mining, quarrying, and oil and gas extraction: 3.9%
  • Health care and social assistance: 3.8%
  • Transportation and Warehousing: 3.8% Footnote Footnote 10 

Businesses from some of these sectors that opted to file an insolvency proceeding in 2024 also increased. These increases ranged from 66.7% in the health care and social assistance sector, 58.6% in the transportation and warehousing sector and 32.7% in the educational services sector.

Definitions
Affiliates:
Companies that are subsidiaries of the filing company or that are controlled by the same person as the filing company.
Bankruptcy:
The state of a consumer or a business that has made an assignment in bankruptcy or against whom a bankruptcy order has been made.
Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act (BIA):
A federal act that provides the framework to file and administer insolvencies in Canada.
Business:
Any commercial entity or organization other than an individual, or an individual who has incurred 50% or more of total liabilities as a result of operating a business.
Census Metropolitan Area (CMA):
Each area consists of one or more adjacent municipalities situated around a major urban core. To form a census metropolitan area, the urban core must have a population of at least 100,000. For Statistics Canada's geographical definitions of the CMAs, go to CMA and CA: Detailed definition.
Companies' Creditors Arrangement Act (CCAA):
A federal law allowing insolvent corporations that owe their creditors in excess of $5 million to restructure their business and financial affairs. CCAA proceedings are carried out under supervision of the courts.
Consumer:
An individual with more than 50% of total liabilities related to consumer goods and services.
Corporation:
A commercial entity or organization.
Economic Region (ER):
Standard geographical units created for analysis of regional economic activity. For Statistics Canada's geographical definitions of ERs, go to Economic region (ER).
Individual Business:
An individual who has incurred 50% or more of total liabilities as a result of operating a business.
North American Industry Classification System (NAICS):
System that classifies economic units that have similar production processes in the same industry. This is a supply-based or production-oriented economic concept.
Proposal:
An offer to creditors to settle debts under conditions other than the existing terms. A proposal is a formal agreement under the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act.
