Quintiles are household-weighted, ranked by equivalised household disposable income; OECD-modified units 1 for first adult, .5 additional persons aged 14+, .3 children under 14. Official definition pages and retrieval hashes are cached.

The current downloaded table metadata, footnote 1, independently confirms the OECD-modified income-quintile definition; [metadata and hashes](benchmarks/B2/raw/table_definitions_provenance.json) are preserved from the same ZIP as the values.

Model matching: re-rank households nationally each quarter by cash disposable income divided by those units, using linked member ages and household expansion weights. Cash income minus net interest is a **proxy for full DHEA disposable income**: noncash national-account items, including imputed owner income, are not individually reconciled. The linked age/membership skeleton is inherited. This limits exact source comparability and is disclosed rather than filled with invented income. Thus the reported G3 failure is on the available model income proxy; a fully concept-matched DHEA income allocation remains unvalidated. DHEA values sum to 1,511.381B/year versus the retained 1,511.382B consumption target: the 1M source rounding/vintage difference is recorded without rescaling either series.

Secondary: [SHS 11-10-0223-01](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1110022301), 2023 total current consumption, household before-tax total-income quintiles, **not equivalised**. These are the revised 2026-09-18 release with updated weights; no use of the superseded 76,750 national mean. Model gross-income ranks reconstruct wages and taxed D2/rent receipts before tax, retain transfers and deposit interest. Differences in income concepts remain explicit.

SHS 2023 survey cash/current-expenditure scope differs from 2022 national accounts: owner-occupied imputed rent, financial-services imputations and other national-account adjustments, reference year, coverage and survey reporting. The model cannot isolate executed imputed housing services from its sector-L bundle. The imputed-rent memo is not a separately identified goods purchase, so it is **not subtracted** to fabricate SHS-comparable shares. Owner-imputed rent, where included, raises upper-quintile shares relative to SHS. Raw levels below are descriptive only, never a gate or a rescaling target.

SHS secondary shares normalise the five published quintile means, treating quintiles as equal household fifths. Table 11-10-0223 publishes means, not quintile household counts; this is an approximation to aggregate expenditure shares. The published all-household mean differs slightly from the mean of the five quintile means. DHEA primary shares instead use published aggregate values directly. No survey weights or expenditure levels are adjusted.

## Remaining household and firm limitations

[Q12 decile × asset-band diagnosis](outputs/B2/constraint_diagnosis_Q12.csv) separates persistent planned-spending gaps with asset drawdown from cases where restoring initial income would cover the budget, and retains combined cases. “Income restoration sufficient” is a counterfactual accounting classification, not causal identification: wages, employment and firm receipts interact. Detailed income changes and cuts are retained; they are not explained away by the aggregate saving match.

B2 removes the large additive-intercept clipping but still gives a steep consumption distribution and rising constraints in the base. Further household behavioural specification requires review; no buffer coefficient is selected from the sensitivities to pass the gates. E remains needed conceptually because negative net worth is not payment default, but implementing it now would mix that change with failed spending-distribution and firm-data gates.

Firm losses after CFC cannot all be labelled behavioural. Unresolved source-zero producers, IO/tax valuation, aggregate CFC concordance, non-residential ownership boundaries and financing remain data/concept limitations. Conditional on those inputs, price noise, demand/employment feedback, the payment-default trigger and continued production/restructuring also affect losses. The CFC-only comparison identifies a data-package effect, not a full causal decomposition. Do not declare household losses, firm losses or bank recapitalisations plausible from accounting closure alone. **The model is not ready for Phase B.**

### Distribution measurement correction

The first quintile diagnostic ranked regional aggregate balances rather than income per household. This affected national comparisons across regions with different expansion factors. Commit d40e353 divides rank income by each region’s factor, retaining the household weights and aggregate consumption. All 15 twelve-quarter configurations were replayed serially with only the corrected read-only diagnostic, requiring GDP, consumption, receipts, constraints, cash DI, household write-offs and bank losses to match every original region/quarter at 1e-12 relative tolerance. Original misranked CSVs are retained for provenance and are **not used for the gate**. Corrected source-specific distributions, replay comparisons and hashes are in [distribution_replays](outputs/B2/distribution_replays_v2). No model parameter or macro path changed.
